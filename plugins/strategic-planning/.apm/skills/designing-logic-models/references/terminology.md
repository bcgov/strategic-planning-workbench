## Overview

In public sector strategy, program evaluation, international development, and product delivery, causal planning methods share common ground but serve distinct operational needs. They form a family of related methods, not exact synonyms.

---

## Closest Synonyms

These terms refer to the core left-to-right causal sequence connecting resources and activities to deliverables and progressive results:

| Term | Typical Context | Meaning & Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Logic model** | Programs, public services, performance evaluation | Maps inputs, activities, outputs, and progressive outcomes (immediate, intermediate, long-term). |
| **Program logic model** | Government, health, education, non-profit | A logic model specifically scoped to a defined public program, service, or grant portfolio. |
| **Results chain** | International development, strategy, performance | Emphasizes how discrete activities and deliverables contribute to a hierarchy of outcomes and impact. |
| **Results framework** | Portfolio and program management | Organizes expected results into a tiered hierarchy (impact, outcomes, outputs) frequently bound to KPI indicators. |
| **Outcome chain** | Benefits realization, evaluation, policy design | Emphasizes the temporal sequence of outcomes: from immediate capacity shifts to intermediate behavior and long-term societal changes. |
| **Results pathway / Impact pathway** | Sustainability, social research, policy evaluation | Traces the causal route through which interventions influence durable long-term conditions. |
| **Causal pathway** | Evaluation science, health research, public policy | Explicitly details the hypothesized cause-and-effect mechanisms connecting intervention variables to outcomes. |
| **Program theory** | Academic evaluation, program audit | The underlying social or behavioral rationale explaining *how* and *why* a program is hypothesized to work. |
| **Intervention logic** | European public administration, policy evaluation | The structured rationale connecting recognized public needs, policy actions, outputs, and direct/indirect effects. |

## Related but Non-Interchangeable Frameworks

| Framework | Core Question | Distinct Distinction from a Standard Logic Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Theory of change (ToC)** | *Why should this intervention cause change?* | Adds explicit causal explanation for *why* transitions occur, including underlying assumptions, preconditions, institutional actors, and external influences. |
| **Logical framework (Logframe)** | *How will results be verified and managed?* | Converts the causal chain into a 4x4 measurement matrix linking narrative summaries to measurable indicators, means of verification, and critical assumptions. |
| **Benefits dependency network (BDN) / Benefits realization map** | *How do enablers and business changes generate value?* | Connects enabling technology changes and business process shifts directly to business benefits and investment objectives. |
| **Strategy map** | *How do strategic objectives reinforce one another?* | Maps strategic objectives across perspectives (financial/stewardship, customer/citizen, internal processes, learning/growth) without detailing inputs, daily tasks, or unit outputs. |
| **Value driver tree** | *What levers drive our primary economic or volume value?* | Decomposes strategic or financial performance into mathematical and operational drivers rather than a chronological causal sequence. |
| **Business capability map** | *What must the organization be able to do?* | Catalogs organizational capabilities and maturity levels (`CAP-X`), independent of specific project timelines or causal intervention chains. |
| **Objectives hierarchy** | *How do operational goals roll up to executive missions?* | Nests operational objectives within tactical and strategic goals without mapping resource inputs or causal transitions. |
| **North Star metric tree** | *What input metrics feed our primary user value metric?* | Connects leading product metrics into an aggregate index, rather than tracing end-to-end program delivery to public impact. |

## Framework Comparison Matrix

| Framework Dimension | Logic Model | Theory of Change | Logframe Matrix | Benefits Map | Strategy Map |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary purpose** | High-level causal sequence | Causal explanation & rationale | Measurement & contract verification | Investment benefits realization | Strategic objective coherence |
| **Input & activity detail** | High (what we invest & do) | Moderate (focus on leverage points) | Moderate to high | Low to moderate | None (objectives only) |
| **Output / Deliverables** | High (controlled deliverables) | Moderate (enablers) | High (contractual outputs) | High (enabling deliverables) | None |
| **Assumptions exposure** | Moderate | High (central focus) | High (risk & preconditions) | Moderate | Low |
| **Indicators & verification** | Optional (measurement depth) | Optional | Mandatory (columns 2 & 3) | Moderate (benefit measures) | High (KPIs on objectives) |

## Selecting the Appropriate Framework

- Choose a **Logic model** when you need a clear, intuitive 1-page visual of what an initiative invests, produces, and expects to change.
- Choose a **Theory of change** when stakeholders disagree on *how* or *why* change happens, or when external risks and deep assumptions are critical.
- Choose a **Results framework / Logframe** when preparing accountability agreements, funding submissions, or formal audit baselines.
- Choose a **Benefits map** when justifying capital IT or transformation investments against organizational benefit registers.
- Choose a **Strategy map** when communicating divisional objective alignment across leadership tiers.
